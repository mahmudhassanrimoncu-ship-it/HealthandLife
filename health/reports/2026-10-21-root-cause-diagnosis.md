## The short answer

There is probably no single hidden disease. The records and story point to **several problems stacked on top of each other, each feeding the others**, ranked here by how strongly the evidence supports them.

1. **The bathroom problem is most likely OCD that has moved into the body.** The core OCD feeling is "incompleteness": something is not finished, not clean, not right. Years of long straining have very likely trained the pelvic floor muscles to tighten when they should relax, so the muscles now really block full emptying, which feeds the feeling. Washing improved on medicine; this did not, because medicine alone rarely fixes it. It needs exposure and response prevention (ERP) and probably pelvic floor retraining. Neither has really been tried.
2. **Fatigue, sleepiness and brain fog have several causes at once.** Biggest suspects: medicines (fluvoxamine at the maximum dose for two years, risperidone and trihexyphenidyl for over a year, now two SSRIs together), broken sleep, depression, and 1 to 3 hours a day of straining. Still to check: orthostatic intolerance / POTS, and deficiencies (vitamin D, B12, iron, thyroid, sodium).
3. **"One good day, then 3 to 7 days of collapse" is real, not laziness.** It is the pattern of running at 100% on a tank that holds about 30%. All-or-nothing routines keep triggering the crash.
4. **Underneath is a long story of shame, ridicule and lost direction** (class 1 accident, harsh father, being laughed at for wanting to be a scientist, family ridicule, repeated failure). That keeps OCD and depression alive and makes anime/phone the main escape from distress.

Hope: on the Rajshahi trip in 2023 bathroom time dropped a lot, and 7-day sprints have worked (CU admission, SSC). A nerve or structural disease would not improve on a trip. A learned mental and muscular loop can.

## Act soon (next 1 to 2 weeks)

Do not stop or change medicines yourself. Take this to Prof. Huq (1-month follow-up was due around 5 Oct) and a medicine doctor.

1. **Fainting and the Eid ul-Adha 2024 episode.** Fainting after straining is usually defecation syncope (a reflex BP drop); "TV static" vision during blood draws fits that reflex. But the Eid episode (memory loss, walked two rooms, soiled) plus a brother with a seizure needs checking. Ask for an ECG, lying and standing BP, and whether an EEG / neurology referral is needed. Until then: rise slowly from squatting, sit or lie down when vision greys out, do not lock the bathroom door.
2. **Rectal bleeding.** Usual causes at 22 are a fissure or haemorrhoids from straining, but both SSRIs reduce platelet function, and there have been three nosebleeds since 2023. Ask for a rectal examination and CBC with ferritin; tell the psychiatrist.
3. **Two SSRIs together** (fluvoxamine 200 mg + fluoxetine 40 mg). Fluoxetine slows fluvoxamine clearance, so levels can rise over 4 to 6 weeks. First-day cramps, sphincter spasms, urgent bowel movements and heavy sweating are known serotonin-excess effects. Ask how this is being monitored.
4. **Falling asleep sitting up** is a safety risk on the commute. Somnolence affects about 1 in 5 people on fluvoxamine in trials.

**Emergency the same day (999)** if: fainting with injury, fainting without warning or while lying down, heavy rectal bleeding or black tarry stools, or heavy sweating together with fever, fast heartbeat, twitching, shaking or confusion. If suicidal thoughts return, tell someone that same day.

## Why every routine has failed

Each routine asks for a full day of energy from a body with about a third of a day available. The loop:

1. Shame and pressure (family, failure, ridicule)
2. All-or-nothing plan ("change my whole life")
3. One full day, far above real capacity
4. Crash: 3 to 7 days, exhausted, foggy, in bed
5. Escape: anime, phone, sleep
6. Missed classes and exams, "proof" of failure → back to 1

Underneath it all is a small energy tank (bathroom 1 to 3 hrs a day, broken sleep, sedating medicines, little sun or movement) that makes every crash bigger. The only step fully in your control is the plan: doing about half of what feels possible on a good day, every day, and stopping before feeling done. To be designed after the tests.

Honest point: anime during study time and skipping study the night before exams are also the way distress is escaped, and they cost you. No shame, but they belong on the list of things to change.
